# Brief: Do retrieval-augmented LLMs hallucinate less than closed-book LLMs, and under what conditions does RAG fail to prevent hallucination?

question `q1` · status: open · uncertainty: high

## ✅ Supported (7)

- **Closed-book LLMs relying only on parametric knowledge hallucinate frequently on knowledge-intensive tasks, with rates as high as 58% on legal queries and systematic falsehoods that mimic human misconceptions.** (c1, confidence 0.90 (author-stated), 3 independent sources)[^1][^2][^3]
- **Retrieval augmentation substantially reduces hallucination relative to closed-book generation across knowledge-grounded dialogue, open-domain QA, and domain applications such as healthcare and education.** (c2, confidence 0.85 (author-stated), 6 independent sources)[^4][^5][^6][^7][^8][^9]
- **RAG's advantage over purely parametric knowledge is largest for less-popular, long-tail factual knowledge, where closed-book models cannot rely on memorization of training data.** (c3, confidence 0.75 (author-stated), 2 independent sources)[^10][^11]
- **RAG does not eliminate hallucination: retrieval-augmented LLMs retain substantial residual hallucination, and standard RAG pipelines can be beaten by multi-evidence refinement by over 40% on hallucination rate.** (c4, confidence 0.80 (author-stated), 4 independent sources)[^12][^13][^14][^15]
- **RAG fails when retrieval quality is poor: irrelevant, noisy, or incorrect retrieved documents (negative retrieval) and weak retriever/embedding components mislead the generator and degrade factual accuracy.** (c5, confidence 0.80 (author-stated), 3 independent sources)[^16][^17][^18]
- **RAG also fails at the generation stage even when retrieval succeeds: models struggle to integrate external truth that conflicts with parametric knowledge and lose relevant evidence positioned in the middle of long contexts.** (c6, confidence 0.75 (author-stated), 3 independent sources)[^19][^20][^21]
- **Part of closed-book hallucination stems from training-data properties - memorization limits, data imbalance (knowledge overshadowing), and imitation of human falsehoods - so mitigation must address both parametric and retrieval-side causes.** (c8, confidence 0.70 (author-stated), 3 independent sources)[^22][^23][^24]

## ⚖️ Contested (1)

- **External retrieval is the only effective way to reduce LLM hallucination.** (c7, confidence 0.15 (author-stated), 2 independent sources)[^25][^26]
